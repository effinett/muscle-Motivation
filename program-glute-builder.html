12-Week Glute Builder

Build a stronger, more sculpted lower body. Every session hits the glutes directly — through heavy compounds, targeted isolation, and smart progression across 12 weeks.

Start Here — Week 1

Your first-week checklist

Glute development takes consistency and patience. Get these foundations in place before anything else.

  • Read the Philosophy and 12-Week Arc so you understand what each phase demands.
  • Check your dashboard for your calorie and macro targets — decide whether you're in a deficit or surplus using the Nutrition section.
  • Pick your schedule (3 or 4 days) based on your dashboard-recommended split.
  • Take your Day 1 baseline: weight, front/side/back photos, and hip and glute measurements.
  • In Week 1, use lighter weights than you think you need — especially on Hip Thrust and RDL. Learn the movement first, load it second.
  • Set up a lift log for your three lead lifts (Hip Thrust, Romanian Deadlift, Squat). You'll track these week to week as your primary progress markers.
  • Focus on feeling the glutes work. If you can't feel a movement in the target muscle, reduce the weight and fix the technique before adding load.
Philosophy

The principles this is built on

  • Glutes respond to consistent direct work. Unlike most muscle groups, the glutes are undertrained in most people's everyday movement. They need to be deliberately targeted every session to grow.
  • Heavy compounds build the foundation. Hip Thrust, Romanian Deadlift, and Squat create the size and strength base. Isolation work refines and sculpts on top of that.
  • Mind-muscle connection is the difference-maker. A glute exercise done without feeling the glutes work is just cardio. Slow down, squeeze, and own every rep.
  • Progressive overload applies here too. Add load or reps over time. Glutes won't grow from the same weight done the same way indefinitely.
  • Consistency beats intensity. Three solid sessions per week for 12 weeks outperforms six brutal sessions that leave you too sore to train again.

The Muscle Motivation approach

Rotate the lead lift, keep the direct work constant. Each session opens with a different primary compound — Hip Thrust, RDL, or Squat — so all three movement patterns get heavy attention across the week. Every session then finishes with direct glute isolation work regardless of which compound led.

Isolation finishers are not optional. Cable kickbacks, abductor work, and glute bridges at the end of each session are where the detail gets built. Don't skip them because you're tired from the compounds.

Volume peaks in Phase 2, quality peaks in Phase 3. Foundation builds the habit and technique. Volume floods the muscle with work. Peak refines the stimulus and maximizes the response from everything you've built.

The 12-Week Arc
Phase 1 — Foundation · Weeks 1–4
Learn the three lead lifts. Build the glute activation habit. Moderate volume, moderate load — focus entirely on technique and feeling the target muscle. Deload Week 4.
Phase 2 — Volume · Weeks 5–8
Volume increases — more sets, more isolation work. Load increases on the compounds. This is where most of the muscle stimulus happens. Push harder, recover carefully. Deload Week 8.
Phase 3 — Peak · Weeks 9–12
Highest quality work. Load and technique are both dialed in. Isolation finishers get extra attention — these are the sessions that produce visible definition. Deload Week 12.
Deload weeks (4, 8, 12): True recovery — reduce load by 30–40% AND cut sets by ~40%. This is lighter than the other programs. Glute tissue is trained every session in this program; it needs genuine recovery to continue growing. Keep the movements, dial back everything else.

Progression Model

Double progression — phase adjusted

Same core model as the other programs, adapted for the phase focus.

Phase 1 · Foundation

  • All movements 10–12 reps
  • Add reps → then add weight
  • Technique is the priority

Phase 2 · Volume

  • Lead lifts: 8–12 reps
  • Add a set to lead lift
  • Isolation work: 12–15 reps

Phase 3 · Peak

  • Lead lifts: 6–10 reps, heavier
  • Isolation: slow tempo, 12–20 reps
  • Hold the squeeze at peak contraction

Intensity (all phases)

  • RPE 7–9 on working sets
  • Leave 1–2 reps in reserve
  • Never rush isolation finishers
The squeeze matters as much as the load. On isolation movements, a slow, controlled rep with a 1-second hold at peak contraction beats a heavier sloppy rep every time. Control is the stimulus.
Nutrition

Use your dashboard targets

Your calorie and macro numbers are personalized on your dashboard. What matters here is choosing the right approach for your goal — building or leaning — and sticking to it consistently.

Protein: 0.9–1.0g per lb of bodyweight. Hit this number daily. It protects muscle while in a deficit and drives growth in a surplus.

Choose your nutrition approach

This program works in either direction. Decide based on your current priority and commit for at least 4 weeks before reassessing.

Lean Out + Build

  • Modest calorie deficit (10–20% below maintenance)
  • Lose fat while building and firming the glutes
  • Scale weight drops gradually
  • Progress shows more in photos and measurements than the scale

Best for: members who want to look leaner while building shape. Results are visible but slower.

Build + Grow

  • Small calorie surplus (200–350 above maintenance)
  • Maximize muscle and strength gains
  • Scale weight rises slowly
  • Fastest route to visible glute development

Best for: members who are already comfortable at their current weight and want to prioritize size and shape.

Whichever you choose: don't under-eat. Glutes are a large, demanding muscle group. Severe restriction slows growth and kills training performance. Eat enough to train hard.

Protein

  • Chicken, turkey, lean beef

  • Greek yogurt, cottage cheese

Reading progress

  • Photos and measurements tell the real story
  • Scale staying flat but shape changing = good
  • Strength on lead lifts trending up = growing
The scale is not the best measure for this program. Building glute muscle while losing fat can keep the scale flat for weeks while your body is dramatically changing shape. Trust the photos, the measurements, and the lift numbers.
Safety & Expectations

What realistic progress looks like

Visible glute development takes time. Expect noticeable shape changes by Week 8 if training and nutrition are consistent — not Week 2. Photos every 4 weeks will show what the mirror can't.

Stop and seek professional guidance if you experience:

  • Sharp pain in the hip, lower back, or knee during Hip Thrust or Squat
  • Numbness or tingling down the legs
  • Pain that worsens across multiple sessions
  • Any joint swelling that doesn't resolve with rest

This program is for educational purposes only and is not medical advice. Consult a qualified physician before beginning any exercise or nutrition program — especially if you have a medical condition, injury history, or are pregnant. Individual results vary based on adherence, genetics, starting point, and nutrition.
